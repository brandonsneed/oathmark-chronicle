Battle 4

The Unquiet Tomb

Regnal Year 200, Spring

Thulemen's Rike had pressed north these past years along the river Aln, and its northernmost holding was but a small hamlet of farms, set at the southern edge of the great forest. At the hamlet's border stood a mound, its sides lined with columns long fallen to ruin, and no soul among the settlers could say to whom it had once belonged.

Finding no claim upon it, and holding as they do a deep reverence for the dead, the colonists took the mound for a shrine, and in the way of their own custom began to leave small offerings there, thinking it a kindness owed to some forgotten soul.

But the mound was no stranger's grave. It was the tomb of an elvish sorcerer-king out of the mythic past, and though the elves are long of memory, even they are not endless, and the turmoil of recent centuries had let this place of devotion slip from their keeping.

Elf spirits are bound to the turning of the natural world in a manner no man can share, and it was the coming of spring, together with the unfamiliar offerings now laid at his tomb, that stirred something in the sleeping king. He woke.

He wanted no worship from men. Reaching out with a sight no longer bound by flesh, he found the beauty of his riverside rest dug up and parceled into ugly little farms, and he was filled with a king's own fury at the insult of it.

What magics he could still command from his disincorporated state, the old king summoned now, and loosed a wailing tempest that lashed out in flesh and in dream alike. A swirling, biting storm bore down upon the hamlet, its eye fixed upon the tomb, and unrelenting bolts of lightning rained for days without cease.

Those attuned to such things felt it first in their sleep, and among them a young sorceress of the Sidhean, who woke dismayed and gathered soldiers from the nearest of her people's hidden holds — a garrison woven into high boughs, perhaps, or sheltered behind some nameless waterfall, or delved into rock no human eye would think to search — determined to drive off the human farmers and see proper veneration restored to their rediscovered lord.

Thulish frontier guards, for their part, were called forth to give battle to the storm itself, and to clear a way for their cleric to purge what they took to be some haunting of the shrine. Neither guard nor priest knew the tempest for what it truly was, nor that the ones who would answer their call were already moving through the trees: the elf warband fell upon them from the woods before the purging could begin.

The human soldiers were wracked by lightning as they pressed past the edge of the hamlet toward their enemy, who stood arrayed at the forest's edge on either side of the tomb. Their priest kept always a pace behind the line, reaching again and again for what power he could summon — and finding, through that long and lightning-wracked day, that little answered him.

As the young sorceress drew near, she felt the ancient king's spirit reach out to her, lending strength and foresight to her craft — yet for all that, she could not calm him in the heat of the fight, and the wind and lightning only worsened, striking elf and man alike without distinction.

As the Sidhean charged a formed rank of human archers, a column of Thule's revenant warriors passed close by the tomb, and the old king's fury reached into several of them and tore their souls from their reclaimed bodies, devouring what it found there.

All the while, unseen, elf hunters worked their way around the hamlet's edge, their hounds held low and quiet, waiting on the signal of the hunt.

At the center, the core of the elvish levy-spears pressed its advance on a company of Thulish thegns, struggling against whipping winds that fought them as hard as any blade.

Then, at the whooping and trumpeting of their huntmasters, the hounds broke from the bushes and hauled half a dozen startled thegns down into the mud. The human right and the elvish spears alike dissolved into chaos at the sight, and both fled for the hills with the hounds in joyful pursuit.

The Thulish frontier guards turned back from the storm to cover their scattered thegns, and fell upon what remained of the elvish spears, routing them entire. Only a single aged elf champion held his ground, a blade dancing in his hands fast enough to turn aside blows from every quarter.

To his aid came the sorceress, her craft now swollen with the dead king's power, and with a blast of raw elements she cut down the last Thuleman of any serious rank left standing on that part of the field.

It was then that the priest, for all his failures that long day, found something at last within himself, and answered her with flame drawn up from the Black Deeps itself. Even the sorceress's storm-drenched robes caught fire, and she was forced to flee the field.

It was the only triumph he would be allowed. An arrow took him in the back a moment later, as the elf rangers came around from their work — for they had, by then, banished what remained of the revenant spirits from the field entire. It was said afterward, in the halls of Thulemen's Rike, that his post had owed rather more to family than to any true gift for the craft — small comfort, now, to the thegns he had failed to shield. With that, all resistance among the men was spent.

The elf warband set about the destruction of the farms that had so offended their rediscovered lord, and before the tomb, in the last light of that long storm, they began the proper rites of appeasement he had been denied for so many centuries.

Aftermath

Srath-Sidhean

When at last the proper rites were sung and the offerings renewed, the old king's fury guttered, and his spirit sank back gently into the earth of his own tomb, no longer raging but merely resting, as a king should. Where his power settled into the soil, the forest came on faster than any forest rightly should, swallowing the ruined farms within a single turning of the moon. And upon the mound itself, and in the damp hollows round about, there sprang up — overnight, it is said — a strange and sudden people: small, quiet folk of living mushroom and moss, grown up out of ground so recently fed by a king's own returning spirit. The Sidhean found these new neighbors already settled, and already reverent of the tomb they guarded unasked. They named them the Fás, and took them gladly under their wing. The little village stands there still, keeping the old king's rest better than any human farm ever could.

Thulemen's Rike

Driven from the hamlet, what remained of Thulemen's frontier command did not long mourn so small a holding — for even as the farms burned, necromancers of the capital were already making their way upriver, drawn by opportunity rather than loss. Theirs is a prestigious art in Thulemen's Rike, and the frontier war, coming as it has on the heels of a hard famine, has given them ground thick with fresh dead and ripe for their purpose. Upon a stark rise above the Aln they raised a tower of dark, unhewn timber — no temple of state, but no less a center of the craft for that — and there the dead of the frontier's wars are brought to be raised again as soldiery of bone, while the souls of the dying nearby are tapped before they can slip beyond reach. It is a rougher work than the ascension rites of the capital's Catacombs, but the kingdom's necromancers prize the posting all the same, for the border has, of late, given them more to work with than any quiet season in the capital ever could.
