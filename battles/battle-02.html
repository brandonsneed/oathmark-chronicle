Battle 2

The Totem of Gabranth's Line

Regnal Year 199, Summer

In the rolling hills where the wastelands of Drosundun tumble down toward gentler country, a convoy of Rabanastre's traders made its way, laden with goods bound for the southern markets, and gave little thought to danger, for the road was a well-worn one.

But Orleg-Skulltaker, a chief of the orc tribes, had set his warband along that road beforehand, and when the convoy entered the tight valley the hills make there, he closed his pincer upon it, and his raiders poured down over the broken ground from both sides at once.

What Orleg-Skulltaker had not reckoned was that the merchants of Rabanastre travel heavy upon that road, and go escorted by guards and scouts enough to answer for it. No sooner had the raiders shown themselves over the pass than they were met with a hail of arrows, and the ambush he had meant to spring became, instead, a fight in earnest.

It was in the thick of that fighting, as one of the king's scouts cast about for cover, that he uncovered, half-buried in the valley floor, a small totem bearing the name of a forefather of King Gabranth himself — a figure spoken of now only in old songs and older oaths.

The discovery turned the battle at a stroke. What had been a fighting retreat became, in a moment, a bitter and determined defense, as Rabanastre's soldiers set themselves to working the totem free of the earth that held it.

Long enough they held the host of Drosundun back to see the relic loaded onto a wagon, though it cost them dear: every guard who stood that line gave his life in the holding of it, and it was the cavalry alone who broke free and rode hard for Rabanastre, bearing grim news of the ambush and an unlooked-for prize both.

News of it reached Rabanastre before the dust of the valley had settled, and the tale of the totem's recovery spread quick through the southern lands, from the king's own halls to the farthest-flung holdings.

Aftermath

Rabanastre

The recovery of so storied a relic did more for Rabanastre than any victory in open battle might have. Word of it traveled far into the desert and the hills, and peoples who had before given King Gabranth no more than wary distance began to come in, one tribe and then another, to swear their loyalty to him — drawn less by the strength of his arms than by the weight of his lineage, now proven in bone and bronze for all to see.

Drosundun

For the orcs of Drosundun, the ambush's failure left Orleg-Skulltaker's name tarnished, but it did not blunt the hunger that sent him onto that road in the first place. If anything, the gleaming cities of men now drew the orcish gaze more fixedly than before. Border towns were put to the torch for their timber, and through the night the smelting fires burned bright and unceasing in the jagged citadels of Drosundun, as the tribes turned their plunder and their envy alike toward the forge.
