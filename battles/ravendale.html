The Ravendale Harvest

Regnal Year 200, Autumn

Introduction

The Grainshires lay at the heart of the Reaches, ringed by kingdoms with far larger armies, and had survived among them for generations. Their freedom was a matter of ledgers. A shire that sold its wheat to Rabanastre one year might sell it to Thulemen's Rike the next, and every treaty its elders sealed was paid for, somewhere in its clauses, in bushels. Outsiders called the shiremen shrewd, and seldom meant it kindly. No great capital could afford to march on them while every great capital was buying.

The famine that struck Thulemen's Rike some seasons past fell harder still upon the plains, whose soil had never been generous to begin with. The shires had always been allies of convenience. Hunger made them inconvenient to one another, and the old understandings failed one by one: a granary withheld here, a border tithe doubled there, an envoy turned away from a gate that had stood open to his kind for a hundred years.

In the middle of all this, Ravendale went quiet. It was a lesser shire, and few noticed at first that its envoys had stopped coming to the councils, or that its roads had emptied. What news escaped was thin and contradictory, and most who heard it assumed the place had burned, or starved, or torn itself apart as others had. Then travelers passing along its borders began to describe fields and vineyards standing green and heavy, in soil that by every account should have failed with the rest.

By the last harvest the stories had grown too loud to ignore, and Ravendale answered them in its own fashion. Its merchants appeared without warning in towns and cities across the plains, their wagons loaded with grain, at a season when the earth elsewhere in the central Reaches gave up almost nothing at all.

They named their prices and little else. Asked where such a harvest had come from, they smiled and spoke of good soil and hard work, and the hungry had no leverage to press them further. When winter closed the roads, Ravendale's traders went home with heavy purses and left a great many questions behind them.

Now another harvest drew near, and it promised no more than the last. A year of hunger had given rumor of Ravendale room to grow, and grow it did. A people who had kept their freedom by being useful and easily forgotten could hardly have drawn worse attention than a shire that prospered through a famine. In Rabanastre and Iceholme, in Thulemen's Rike and the hidden holds of Srath-Sidhean, the same question was being turned over: what was growing in Ravendale, and who would reach it first?
