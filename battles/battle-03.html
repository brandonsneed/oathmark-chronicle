Battle 3

The Storehouse Raid

Regnal Year 199, Winter

Winter had come upon the land, and the orc tribes of Drosndun girded themselves for a hard season, for it is not given to the orc to farm well, and he would rather take by the sword what another has sown than labor for it himself. Now it came to pass that a southern tribe, returning that year from a summer abroad, brought home with them much gold and fine silks, but no store of grain, and nothing laid in against the cold.

To the coastal lands they turned their eyes, which once had yielded easy plunder; but the dominion of Thulemen's Rike had crept steadily along that shore in recent years, and where raiders once ranged unchecked, the tall ships of the Thulemen now kept a stout and watchful order. What was meant as a quiet ambush would become, instead, a pitched and bloody reckoning.

The raiders came down from the hills under cover of darkness, and went carefully around the scattered farms and the roads, that the coastal village might have no warning of what approached. But it so happened that the village had, that night, a visitor: a deadspeaker priest, whose blind eyes saw what living eyes could not, and espied the warband a mile off. At his word the village rose, the living and the dead together, and made ready to meet them.

Thus the ambush became a battle. Drosndun's soldiery came on through the heart of the village, while a column of their spears labored at the stone fencing on the far side of the town. The chief himself led his hearthguard over the western hill, where the way to the storehouses lay open and few barricades of man's making stood between him and his prize. There, instead, waited a formed rank of raised skeletal spearmen, spears leveled in the open ground.

At the village center the town militia stood before their own hearths, and were not alone, for the deadspeaker's chanting steadied them, and at his back stood a guard of undead bowmen, drawn up to loose at his command.

The host's advance began with a great bellow, though it was slowed somewhat by the village fencing, for the greater part of their strength had chosen instead to press the flanks. The deadspeaker made full use of the delay, and fell upon the oncoming raiders with a hail of magic; then, in the very moment they would have closed to the charge, he called water up out of the earth itself, and the well-trodden ground at the village center became a thick and clinging mire, and put the orc chief and his hearthguard off their feet just as the charge was to be sprung.

The raised dead, caring nothing for mud nor mire, marched on into it all the same, and on the western side of the village a brawl broke out that held no shape nor order.

Elsewhere, the rest of the host gave a howl and came on heedless of the arrows raining upon them, and fell upon the assembled warriors of the village. Blow was given for blow in a long and bloody reckoning, and though the men never once gave way, and though the raiders' dead piled high before them, still they were pressed back, step by step, to the rear boundary of the village.

By now the deadspeaker's power was strained near to its limit, and the dead ranks in his service crumbled to dust even as they fought; so he turned what magic remained to him upon the living, and new strength filled the warriors of the village, who held their ground and did not flee. Yet it was the raiders who had the better of the day, for their rearguard and camp-followers had crept in unseen behind the lines, and fell upon the village storehouses, and put them to the sack.

At the sound of a horn the warband broke off and made for the hills, and the defenders, spent by the long fight, could not muster the strength to answer them. So the orcs returned to the dark hills of Drosndun, laden with food and plunder enough to see them through to the spring.

Aftermath

Drosndun

Orc society in Drosndun knows no single master; many are the chiefs and lords who claim descent from kings, and many are their ambitions. It came to pass this season that a northern tribe, close kin to the ruling Tyrant, found itself fallen from favor at court, and has taken up a migratory expedition into the fertile woodlands of the west. Unity among the orcs has thus waned, and any warband raised in the near term will be the smaller for it — yet should this new expansion prosper, the power and prestige of all Drosndun shall wax again. At home, the smoke billows late from the smithies, and timber is felled wherever it may be found, for industrial arts long forgotten are being learned anew; and the High Tyrant, for his part, grows weary of petty raiding, calling it a war-craft far beneath the noble blood of Herkir.

Thulemen's Rike

It has been a turbulent season in Thulemen's Rike, a young kingdom built of old souls, whose patchwork territories are only now beginning to knit together. The orc raid won no ground of lasting worth, yet it carried off or destroyed grain enough that hunger is plainly felt in the cities, and both native and Thulemen alike must settle in for a lean and barren winter, with grain selling dear. The risen dead, knowing no hunger, labor on unchanged; and it was this same season that the priests of Thule consecrated at last their divine royal halls — towering, half-pyramidal mounds raised to house the preserved bodies of the kings of Thule, their aldermen, and those warriors granted, by great esteem, the right of ascension. It is counted among the highest of rites to call these spirits back out of the earth, that they might rise and fight again at the sides of their sons.
