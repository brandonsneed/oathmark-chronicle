Battle 1

Clash at the Fountain Crossroads

Regnal Year 199, Spring

To the west of Srath-Sidhean the high hills rise, and beneath them run tunnels and galleries older than either elf or dwarf can rightly claim. No border had ever been drawn through that dark, for it is an easy thing to mark a boundary upon open ground, and a hard thing indeed to mark one in the deep places of the earth, where no soul can say with certainty whose digging came first.

It was into this uncertain dark that a company of Srath-Sidhean's tunnel scouts went, ranging as they were accustomed, and at a crossroads where four passages met about a small and ancient fountain — older, by the look of its stone, than either kingdom's claim to the ground above — they came upon a force of Iceholme's dwarves, likewise ranging, and likewise certain the ground beneath their feet was their own.

Words were exchanged before blades were, as is often the way of such meetings, but the dwarves are not known for patience, nor are the elves strangers to pride, and it was not long before hot tempers got the better of cooler counsel. Among the Sidhean rode a sorceress, and when the dwarves pressed their claim too hotly for her liking, she answered them with fire.

The ground about the fountain favored neither side, open as it was, with the four tunnel-mouths offering equal cover to both, and so it fell to a plain test of arms, fought close and hard around the old stone basin. The sorceress's fire caught the fountain's spray as it fell, and turned the crossroads to billowing steam as thick as smoke, until elf and dwarf alike swung more by sound and instinct than by any sight of the other.

When at last the steam cleared, it was Iceholme who broke first: their captain, battered near past standing, was carried from the field by his own kin, as the dwarves fell back into the deep they had come from.

Aftermath

Srath-Sidhean

Srath-Sidhean, emboldened, set in earnest to the deepening of its own holdings beneath the hills. Among the new works raised in that dark are kennels built to house the elves' hunting hounds — for scouts alone, it seems, were not swift enough to answer the next incursion, and a border that cannot be drawn with stone or treaty may yet be watched by keener noses than any elf's own.

Iceholme

Word of the retreat traveled hard through Iceholme's halls, and did not sit well there. The dwarves had kept largely to their stone through the age now passing, content to let the world above sort its own quarrels while they delved and built in peace below. That peace was broken at the fountain crossroads, and the outrage of it has pushed Iceholme's rulers toward a sterner course than has long been their custom — the patience of the mountain, it seems, has its limits, and Srath-Sidhean may yet find a harder neighbor beneath its hills than it bargained for.
