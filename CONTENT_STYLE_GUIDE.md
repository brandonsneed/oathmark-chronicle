# Oathmark Chronicle — Content Style Guide

Calendar: regnal year of the Elf Queen (currently unnamed — substitute her name once chosen).
Max one battle per season typically (Spring/Summer/Autumn/Winter), winter least likely. Some years get extra/out-of-sequence battles, including multi-battle sequences (e.g. military expansions).

## Kingdoms
- Iceholme — Dwarves
- Srath-Sidhean — Elves
- Thulemen's Rike — Men and Undead
- Drosundun — Orcs
- Rabanastre — Men

## Rules

1. **Voice.** Third person, past tense, authoritative. ~15% King James Bible lift — "it came to pass," syntactic inversion, "for"/"yet" as connectors, elevated but plain-spoken diction. No deliberate rhyme or forced cadence. Not inaccessible — clear and sophisticated, not pastiche (avoid thee/thou/"loins"-level archaism).

2. **Page structure.** Header (Battle #, title, Regnal Year + Season) → two columns:
   - **Narrative** (left): prose only. Never states stats, territory, or results directly.
   - **Technical** (right): bare facts only, no prose. Fields: Combatants, Scenario, Outcome, Territory Gained, Territory Occupied, Kingdom Events.
   Then **Aftermath** (full-width, below both columns): one solid prose paragraph per involved faction, explaining what the Technical facts mean in-world. Narrative and Technical never cross over — no stats in prose, no story in the sidebar.

3. **Length.** Target Battle 3 ("The Storehouse Raid") as the density benchmark: several narrative paragraphs carrying setup → contact → climax → resolution, plus one full paragraph per faction in Aftermath. Do not invent facts to pad thin source notes. Instead, flag the specific gap (missing tactical detail, unclear consequence, ambiguous field, etc.) and let the author pencil it in.

4. **Epithet rotation.** Name each faction plainly on first use per battle, then rotate through kingdom-derived or role-based alternatives rather than repeating the species noun every time.
   - Drosundun orcs → raiders / the warband / the host / Drosundun's soldiery
   - Thulemen's Rike undead (skeletal) → raised skeletal spearmen / undead bowmen (first use, for clarity) → the risen dead / the raised dead / the dead ranks
   - Other kingdoms: apply the same principle lightly as needed (first use plain, then vary) — only orcs/skeletons have been explicitly flagged as grating so far; don't over-rotate kingdoms that haven't shown the problem.

5. **Outcome field, divergent results.** When the battlefield result and the strategic/narrative result point different ways, use a compound Outcome line:
   `[tactical result] (field) · [strategic result] ([cause])`
   Example: "Narrow orc victory (field) · fortuitous strategic gain for Rabanastre (totem recovered)"

6. **Unnamed characters.** Fine and expected when players haven't named them yet. Never invent names to fill the gap — leave them as role-described (e.g. "the sorceress," "the dwarf captain").

7. **Default pronoun.** Default to male pronouns for unnamed combatants unless a character is specifically established as female. (No female characters are currently in use across the chronicle; avoid introducing gendered ambiguity for the reader.)
